# Preview eligibility, current display and retention

Requirements §6.11, D13/D16; producers WX-08/19/38, consumers WX-20/33 and cleanup/custody WX-18/30. The [strict v1 schema](preview-policy.schema.json) resolves the product configuration's `camera_previews.retention` policy reference. It covers the selected preview behavior, not the complete private deployment or recovery bundle. All values are explicit; no JSON Schema default silently enables production behavior. A changed behavior requires a new versioned recipe and admission review within the authorized implementation scope.

## Three separate decisions

1. **Earliest publication:** the latest plausible exposure time plus at least 600 seconds must be <= trusted authorization time. Unknown timing or unsafe clock state rejects publication. `public_not_before` expresses that earliest instant, not cache freshness.
2. **Current display:** selected best source timestamp remains immutable. Through 1,200 seconds of age the pointer may be fresh; above 1,200 through 1,800 it is stale; above 1,800 the latest-preview response is unavailable with a reason and no asset URLs. A new HTTP response, ETag, pointer generation or publication timestamp never resets source age. If the timestamp uncertainty spans a freshness cutoff, use the older plausible exposure bound to avoid labeling potentially older imagery fresh. This is conservative classification, not a claim of exact exposure time.
3. **Logical retention:** source-based 86,400-second horizon determines when this preview derivative is no longer eligible for historical direct public access. `Image.expires_at` refers to that logical asset expiry, **not** the 1,800-second current-display cutoff and **not** physical object deletion. A historical eligible derivative may remain accessible after the latest pointer becomes a placeholder. Physical deletion additionally requires all references, pins, recovery checkpoints and owner lifecycle rules to permit it.

At exactly 1,200 seconds classify fresh, exactly 1,800 classify stale, and at the logical expiry instant deny access (half-open public lifetime). Earliest publication equality is eligible. Safety withdrawal may deny access sooner than natural expiry; never extend retention through pointer refresh. Asset authorization must evaluate current expiry/withdrawal before HEAD, range or conditional 304 responses. Cache withdrawal delay is still an unresolved D14/D16 production qualification; the 15-second pointer cache bound does not make immutable browser copies revocable.

## Binding and immutable policy revisions

Resolve and hash-check the referenced policy from approved custody. Require its policy ID and namespace/environment binding to match the effective configuration. The initial v1 product values `minimum_publication_age_seconds` and `refresh_seconds` must equal the resolved policy's 600 and 60; disagreement is a startup/configuration error, not min/max guessing. More conservative future embargo changes require a compatible new policy version and revised expiry/freshness semantics. This v1 schema intentionally does not pretend arbitrary numeric changes are already qualified.

Resolve clock qualification, retention/recovery and resource-admission references independently. Hash equality does not establish owner authorization or clock health. Check the selected capture epoch, source uncertainty, admitted durable input/derivative byte budgets and exact effective policy revision. Freeze the relevant revision in every derivative and pointer publication. A privacy-tightening policy change immediately blocks newly disallowed origin access and triggers controlled cache/projection invalidation; it does not rewrite the source capture time. Existing expired generations cannot be resurrected by rollback.

## Queue, handoff and loss semantics

One pending candidate per camera coalesces to the newest eligible committed capture. Retain only that candidate's bounded durable working input while needed. Replacement releases only that preview job's pin after ownership transfer/termination is safe; it cannot release hourly, keogram or video pins. Expiry does not prove a crashed job stopped reading. After outage, do not replay every missed preview minute. All three cameras report their own availability.

Persist source time/uncertainty, first publication, logical expiry, generation, policy and source-quality provenance. The public Image's `source_interval`, `exposure_upper_bound`, `first_published_at`, `expires_at`, `state`, `reason` and revision carry the sanitized contract. Current endpoint state is evaluated on each response; asset identity/revision remains immutable. A client with an already received preview must age it locally and stop displaying it as current at its conservative cutoff even if subsequent polls fail. The client may become more conservative during connection failure; it cannot extend a server-authorized lifetime. Server authorization remains authoritative for obtaining bytes.

## Acceptance obligations

The [12 shape fixtures](preview-policy-fixtures.json) reject weaker embargo, mutable input, backlog, age resetting, missing clock binding and retention/deletion conflation. They do not execute time decisions or resolve references. Runtime tests must cover each equality and one tick either side of 600/1,200/1,800/86,400; uncertainty spanning a cutoff; future/unknown clock; cached pointer while time advances; failed polling; unchanged bytes served repeatedly; latest placeholder with still-eligible historical asset; physical recovery hold after logical expiry; pending replacement with another product's pin; privacy-tightening policy reload; and rollback to a previously expired generation. Negative asset requests include HEAD, Range, conditional 304 and thumbnail aliases. No model, network or camera call is needed for synthetic logic tests.

No runtime or physical deletion is qualified by these schemas. Admission, cache withdrawal, clock evidence and actual expiry/pin enforcement remain implementation/live acceptance work.
